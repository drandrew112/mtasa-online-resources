Only for a "B" nurse. Passing it makes you B+ automatically.

## Dosing

- A per-kg dose is multiplied by the body weight: 80 kg × 1 mcg = 80 mcg.
- Repeated doses of the same medicine add up and can lead to an overdose.
- Without IV: Captopril, Nitroglycerin, Naloxone, Salbutamol, glucose gel. Everything else is IV.

## The medicines

- **Ketamine (Calypsol):** anaesthetic, RSI step 1 (1.5 mg/kg), painkiller in a small dose (0.3 mg/kg). Keeps or raises the blood pressure.
- **Rocuronium (Esmeron):** muscle relaxant, the breathing stops. Only with sedation and intubation. Giving it to an awake patient is a serious error.
- **Fentanyl:** strong painkiller, 1 mcg/kg. Can lower the blood pressure. Overdose: depressed breathing, small pupils; the antidote is Naloxone.
- **Adrenaline (Tonogen):** cardiac arrest 1 mg IV every 3–5 minutes. Peri-arrest low pressure: "push dose" about 0.02 mg. Raises the pulse and the pressure, helps ROSC.
- **Adenosine (Adenocor):** only for an SVT with a pulse. 6 mg fast IV, if it does not work 12 mg. The heart stops for a few seconds (an almost flat line), then restarts. The awake patient feels a short chest pressure. In any other rhythm only the pause shows; no effect in cardiac arrest.
- **Captopril:** 25 mg under the tongue for high blood pressure.
- **Nitroglycerin:** 0.4 mg (1 puff); 2 puffs are already above the usual dose.
- **Glucose 40%:** 40 ml (16 g) IV. **Glucose gel:** 15 g, only if the patient can swallow.
- **Insulin (Actrapid):** slowly lowers a very high glucose. Its main danger is hypoglycaemia: never for an unknown glucose.
- **Naloxone:** 4 mg nasal (2 mg smaller).
- **Salbutamol:** 2.5–5 mg nebulised. A silent chest in asthma: life threatening.
- **Midazolam (Dormicum):** sedative for cocaine / amphetamine agitation and seizures, 0.05 mg/kg. Depressed breathing, low pressure.
- A sleeping pill (benzodiazepine) overdose has no antidote here: airway, oxygen, monitoring.
