Needs B+ and the RSI exam. A short review of the earlier exams (medicines, IV, intubation) and new material.

## Breathing

- **Wheezing, only short words:** severe asthma. **Silent chest:** life threatening.
- **Crackles, swollen ankles, pink frothy sputum:** pulmonary oedema – sit up, Nitroglycerin, oxygen.
- **Deep, fast, acetone smell (Kussmaul):** very high blood glucose.
- **Slow, shallow:** opioid or sleeping pill poisoning.
- **Snoring** in an unconscious patient: partial airway obstruction.
- **Gasping (agonal):** cardiac arrest, start CPR.
- **COPD** (pursed lips, leaning forward): oxygen helps the most.

## ECG rhythms

- **Shockable:** ventricular fibrillation (VF, chaotic waves) and pulseless ventricular tachycardia (wide, regular, fast waves).
- **Not shockable:** asystole (flat line) and PEA (an organised rhythm on the monitor but no palpable pulse).
- Never shock a talking patient in sinus rhythm: the shock can stop the heart.
- **VT with a pulse, unstable patient:** synchronised cardioversion (SYNC). An unsynchronised shock can cause VF.

## CPR

- Shockable rhythm: defibrillate as soon as possible, CPR in between. After a shock, 2 minutes of CPR at once, only then a rhythm check.
- Adrenaline: in a non-shockable rhythm as soon as possible, in a shockable one after the 3rd shock, then every 3–5 minutes.
- Untreated VF turns into asystole within minutes.
- Change the compressor about every 2 minutes. Adrenaline and a secured airway also help ROSC.
- Reversible causes: **4H** (hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypo-/hyperkalaemia, hypothermia), **4T** (tension pneumothorax, tamponade, toxins, thrombosis). Arrest from low glucose: Glucose 40% IV.
- After ROSC: monitor, oxygen, watch the pressure and the rhythm, transport.
