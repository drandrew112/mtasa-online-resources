# Diagnosis: neuro exam, glucose, skin, breathing

Medical conditions (poisoning, stroke, low sugar, asthma...) are not written on the panel. You find them from the signs: the neuro exam, the glucose, the skin, the breathing and the vital signs on the monitor. Put the signs together, like a real medic.

## Neuro exam

**D row – Neuro exam**. It takes 4 seconds, needs no equipment, and the findings stay on the panel (updated live). Colours: white = normal, yellow / orange = abnormal, red = serious.

### Responsiveness (AVPU)

- **Alert and oriented** – fine.
- **Awake but confused, disoriented** – intoxication, sugar, stroke, after a seizure, head injury.
- **Drowsy, responds to voice** – getting worse: watch the airway.
- **Responds to pain only** – unconscious. The airway is at risk.
- **Sedated – not assessable** – you put them to sleep (Ketamine / Rocuronium).
- **Unresponsive, pupils fixed and dilated** – cardiac arrest or death.

### Pupils

- **Equal, react to light** – normal.
- **Pinpoint** – opioid (heroin, fentanyl). Give **Naloxone**.
- **Dilated** – stimulant (cocaine, amphetamine). Give **Midazolam**.
- **Normal size, sluggish / sluggish** – sleeping pills, sedatives. No antidote: support the breathing.
- **UNEQUAL – one is wide and slow** – serious head injury, pressure on the brain. Hospital fast.

### FAST (stroke test)

FAST = **F**ace, **A**rms, **S**peech, **T**ime.

- **FAST: negative** – face, arms and speech normal.
- **FAST: face droops on the left / right side**, **arm drifts down, weak grip**, **speech slurred** – **stroke**. Keep the systolic pressure between 130 and 220 and transport. Only the hospital treats it.
- **Left / Right side paralysed**, **eyes deviated to one side** – severe stroke.
- **FAST: not assessable** – the patient is unconscious.

### Clues

The exam also reports what you notice around the patient:

- **Needle marks on the arms**, **syringe lying next to the patient** – opioid.
- **Empty pill packets nearby**, **no gag reflex** – sedative overdose.
- **Agitated, talks fast**, **jaw clenching, sweating**, **paranoid, hot to the touch** – stimulant.
- **Smells strongly of alcohol**, **vomited, cannot stand**, **vomit around the mouth** – alcohol. Vomit can block the airway.
- **Bitten tongue**, **wet trousers**, **does not remember what happened** – after an epileptic seizure. It wears off by itself.
- **Scalp haematoma**, **asks the same questions again**, **vomited twice**, **blood / clear fluid from the ear** – head injury.
- **Speaks in full sentences / short phrases only / cannot speak** – how bad an asthma attack is.
- **Pursed-lip breathing, long-time smoker** – COPD.
- **Swollen ankles**, **pink frothy sputum** – fluid on the lungs (pulmonary oedema).
- **Sweaty, trembling, pale** – low blood sugar.
- **Fruity (acetone) smell on the breath** – very high blood sugar (ketoacidosis).

## Glucometer

![300](content/images/medical/glucometer.jpg)

**D row – Glucometer** opens the meter next to the panel (it needs the glucometer from the bag). Press **MEASURE**: the finger prick reading comes after 5 seconds. It is a single reading, not a live value: the time since the last measurement is shown under it, so measure again after treatment. Stay close while it measures. **PUT AWAY** closes it.

- The reading is in **mg/dL**, with **mmol/L** under it (mg/dL / 18).
- **LO** = under 20, **HI** = over 600. In cardiac arrest there is no reading (no blood flow).

### Reading the value

- **70–180 mg/dL** – normal (95 healthy).
- **Under 70** – hypoglycaemia: sweaty, pale, fast pulse. Under 58 confused, under 45 dazed, under 30 unconscious. Under 20 for 4 minutes the heart stops. Awake patient: **Oral glucose gel**; unconscious: **Glucose 40%** IV.
- **Over 300** – the patient loses fluid (dry, warm skin, the blood pressure drops). Over 350: deep, rapid breathing and acetone breath (**ketoacidosis**). Over 420 confused, over 550 dazed, over 700 unconscious. Give IV fluids and **Insulin**.

>! Never give Insulin without measuring: with a normal sugar it causes a dangerous hypoglycaemia.

## Skin

Shown on the bleeding tile, always visible (you see it and touch it).

- **Warm, normal** – fine.
- **Pale** – lost some blood (under 85%).
- **Pale, cold, clammy** – lost a lot of blood (under 70%): shock.
- **Pale, sweaty** – low sugar or stimulants.
- **Dry, warm** – very high sugar.
- **Cyanotic** (blue) – SpO2 under 85%: not enough oxygen.
- **Cold, mottled** – dead.

## Breathing

- **Normal**, **Ventilated** (through the tube) – fine.
- **Rapid** – pain, fast pulse, stimulants.
- **Wheezing** – asthma or COPD. **Salbutamol**, O2 mask.
- **Silent chest, exhausted** – life-threatening asthma: almost no air moves.
- **Laboured** / **Laboured, crackles** – SpO2 low; crackles = fluid on the lungs (**Nitroglycerin**).
- **Deep and rapid** – ketoacidosis (very high sugar).
- **Slow, shallow** – opioid or sedative.
- **Snoring** – the tongue blocks the airway of an unconscious patient. Intubate.
- **Gasping** – agonal breathing, the heart is about to stop.
- **Not breathing** – cardiac arrest, or a paralysed patient without a tube.

## Vital signs on the monitor

See [[medical_monitor|Lifepak 15]] for the rhythms. Quick reading:

- **Pulse fast + pressure low + pale** – blood or fluid loss: stop the bleeding, IV fluids.
- **Pressure very high + slow pulse + unequal pupils** – pressure on the brain (head injury).
- **Pressure very high + FAST positive** – stroke.
- **SpO2 low** – the airway or the lungs: O2 mask, Salbutamol / Nitroglycerin, intubation.
